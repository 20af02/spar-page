SPAR: Scene-grounded Pose
And track Refinement

1Robotics Institute, Carnegie Mellon University 2School of Architecture, Carnegie Mellon University

Overview of SPAR. Per-frame human mesh recovery methods such as PromptHMR place bodies well in the image but often get depth and pose wrong under occlusion and close contact. SPAR refines PromptHMR's predictions over time. Points on the PromptHMR mesh act as tracks that attend to scene tokens from a frozen VGGT-Ω encoder and to learned shape tokens. On occluded frames, a corrector fine-tuned from ReLift adjusts the points.

Approach

Method. SPAR takes PromptHMR's per-frame meshes and SAM 3.1 person masks as input. We sample points on each mesh and follow them through time, and each point carries its position, velocity and acceleration. A frozen VGGT-Ω encoder gives scene tokens for every frame. A shape encoder adds 64 tokens per frame inside VGGT-Ω with a one-way attention mask, so the frozen features stay unchanged while the shape encoder still gets gradients. A transformer decoder lets the point tracks attend to these tokens over 12-frame windows and predicts refined 3D points. On occluded frames, a corrector fine-tuned from ReLift adjusts the points.
